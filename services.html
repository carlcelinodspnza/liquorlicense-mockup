Services 01 to 04

Moving a licence between owners

The four services that change who holds a California licence, and what it is worth on the day they do.

Service 01

Buy a liquor licence

We source off-market Type 47, 48 and 21 licences inside California's limited secondary market, then examine each one as an asset rather than as a document: liens registered against it, disciplinary history sitting on its file, and whether it can lawfully be transferred to you at all. A licence that survives that becomes an acquisition we manage end to end, from the letter of intent through to final issuance by the ABC.

What this covers

  • Off-market sourcing across a limited secondary market
  • Type 47, 48 and 21 assets vetted before an offer
  • Liens, disciplinary history and transferability checked
  • Acquisition run from letter of intent to final ABC issuance
Browse live inventory

Service 02

Sell a liquor licence

A licence is a business asset, and it sells like one. We connect sellers with buyers who have already been qualified — institutional groups and independent operators both — so that the people making offers are the people able to complete. The sale is secured in escrow, and the brief throughout is the highest achievable market price for the licence rather than the quickest available exit.

What this covers

  • Access to pre-qualified institutional and independent buyers
  • The licence positioned as a business asset
  • Escrow secured before any funds move
  • Held to the highest achievable market price
Selling questions answered

Service 03

Transfer a liquor licence

Two different movements share the same word. A person-to-person transfer puts the licence in a new owner's name at the same address; a premises-to-premises transfer takes it to a new address. We streamline both, which in practice means working outside our own office as much as inside it: with local law enforcement on the background side, and with state investigators on the application side. The object is downtime — keeping the parts that can run in parallel running in parallel, so trading is interrupted as little as the process allows.

What this covers

  • Person-to-person transfer phases
  • Premises-to-premises transfer phases
  • Coordination with local law enforcement
  • Coordination with state investigators
  • Downtime minimised across the whole schedule
See how a transfer runs

Service 04

Licence valuation

An appraisal is only as good as the transactions underneath it. Ours are real-time, built on transactions closing now rather than on a historic average, because a licence does not hold a fixed value: prices move month to month with county demand and with legislative change. Owners take the figure into legal filings, into partnership buyouts, and into strategic financial planning where the licence is one of the larger numbers on the page.

What this covers

  • Real-time appraisals from current closed transactions
  • Written for legal filings
  • Written for partnership buyouts
  • Written for strategic financial planning
  • Re-run as county demand and legislation move

Services 05 to 08

The work that sits around the transaction

Permits, compliance and escrow are how a licence stays clean once it is yours. The fourth service here runs before there is a licence, or a business, at all.

Service 05

Conditional Use Permits

These are decided in a room rather than on a form. We secure the Conditional Use Permits and police permits a site needs, and we represent the business wherever that decision is actually made: at the planning-commission hearing, and at the neighbourhood council meetings that shape what a commission hears before it sits. We do that work across California's major cities, where the local politics of a new licence are usually the harder half. Where local approval falls inside a transaction is set out on the process page.

  • Conditional Use Permits secured
  • Police permits secured
  • Representation at planning-commission hearings
  • Representation at neighbourhood council meetings
  • Covered across California's major cities
When local approval happens

Service 06

ABC compliance consulting

A violation costs far less to prevent than to answer. We run operational audits on how the premises actually trades rather than how the manual says it does, put staff through LEAD-program training, and keep premises compliance current. All of it is managed proactively and on a schedule: the point is that the file stays clean, not that we are good at defending it once it is not.

  • Operational audits of how the premises trades
  • LEAD-program staff training
  • Premises compliance kept current
  • Managed proactively, ahead of a costly ABC violation
Ask about an audit

Service 07

Escrow and transaction guidance

Both sides of a deal need one desk that belongs to neither of them. That is our role here: transaction funds handled independently and securely, every statutory notice filed, and disbursements made in the compliant order. In practice it is also the answer to the question buyers, sellers, lenders and lawyers all ask on the same afternoon — where is this transaction right now. What the statute itself demands of an escrow, and why it exists at all, belongs to the process page.

  • Transaction funds held independently
  • Secure handling from deposit through to close
  • All statutory notices filed
  • Compliant disbursements
  • One point of contact for both sides and their advisers
What the statute requires

Service 08

New business licence planning

Illustrative scope, written by the design team and awaiting the client's confirmation. This is the only service on the page with no source text behind it: the client's own document repeats the escrow description under this heading, so nothing below is quoted from it.

A concept on paper has a different problem from a trading business. The licence type has to be chosen before a site is signed rather than after; whether that type can even be obtained has to be tested in each county on the shortlist; and the licence, the permit and the build-out have to be sequenced against one another, so that an opening date is not quietly set by the slowest of the three.
