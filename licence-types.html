The classifications

California ABC licence classifications, explained

Five classifications, set out in full. Here is what each one authorises — in the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control's own wording, where that wording exists — and the condition attached to it that decides whether a given site can actually hold it.

The five classifications on this page

Three of them — 21, 47 and 48 — are the classifications behind every licence on our inventory board. Two more, 41 and 20, sit one step below them on scope: each is the beer-and-wine counterpart of a full-spirit classification above it, and neither authorises distilled spirits.

21

Type 21 — Off-Sale General

The off-sale full-spirit classification. Siting turns on distance from schools, churches and existing high-crime zones.

47

Type 47 — On-Sale General, Eating Place

The on-sale full-spirit classification tied to bona fide eating place status, with sales-ratio and operating-hours conditions attached.

48

Type 48 — On-Sale General, Public Premises

The on-sale full-spirit classification for a public premises rather than an eating place. Minors prohibited, and background checks are the high-scrutiny kind.

41

Type 41 — On-Sale Beer & Wine, Eating Place

Beer and wine for on-site consumption at a bona fide eating place. Does not authorise distilled spirits.

20

Type 20 — Off-Sale Beer & Wine

Beer and wine sold for consumption off the premises. Does not authorise distilled spirits.

Reading this as a shopping list is the wrong way round. A classification is not chosen in the abstract — it follows from what the business does, where it does it and who walks in. The homepage sets that out sector by sector.

What separates one classification from the next

Three questions settle it: what you may pour or sell, whether the drink is consumed on your floor or carried out of the door, and the condition the classification carries for as long as you hold it.

Classification What it authorises On- or off-premises The condition that bites
Type 21 — Off-Sale General Spirits included Off-premises Distance from schools, churches and existing high-crime zones
Type 47 — On-Sale General, Eating Place Spirits included On-premises; beer and wine also off Bona fide eating place status, food-to-alcohol sales ratio, operating hours
Type 48 — On-Sale General, Public Premises Spirits included On-premises; beer and wine also off Minors prohibited, high-scrutiny background checks, entertainment conditions
Type 41 — On-Sale Beer & Wine, Eating Place Beer and wine only On-premises Bona fide eating place status; no distilled spirits
Type 20 — Off-Sale Beer & Wine Beer and wine only Off-premises No distilled spirits

A note on the last two rows, made plainly rather than papered over: our verified sources record the official designation and scope for Types 41 and 20, but not the full authorisation sentence recorded for 21, 47 and 48. So those two rows state the designation and the scope, and stop there rather than fill the gap with wording nobody published.

In practice

The three full-spirit classifications, one at a time

Type 21

Off-Sale General

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for consumption off the premises where sold.

The boundary written into that sentence is the door. Everything the licence covers leaves the shop sealed; nothing in it authorises a glass poured on site. Which means the file that decides a Type 21 is rarely the applicant's — it is the address's. Distance from a school, from a church, from an area already carrying a crime problem is weighed before anything else is, and it is why a Type 21 that transfers cleanly into one storefront can stall at another address in the same city.

  • The licence liquor stores hold
  • Off-premises consumption, full spirits included
  • Siting turns on distance from schools, churches and existing high-crime zones
Type 47

On-Sale General, Eating Place

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for on-premises consumption; beer and wine for off-premises consumption. Must maintain bona fide eating place status.

Read the last clause as a standing obligation, not a box ticked once at issuance. Bona fide eating place status has to be held for as long as the licence is held, and the two conditions that sit under it — the food-to-alcohol sales ratio and the operating hours — stay live with it. It is also the classification an eating place needs before a spirits list can go on the menu at all — a Type 41 does not reach that far, so the two are not interchangeable and the difference is a whole classification wide.

  • Full liquor for on-site consumption at a bona fide eating place
  • Carries a food-to-alcohol sales-ratio condition
  • Carries an operating-hours condition
Type 48

On-Sale General, Public Premises

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for on-premises consumption; beer and wine for off-premises consumption. Minors prohibited.

A Type 48 authorises the same drinks as a Type 47 and answers to a different regime, because the room is licensed as a public premises rather than as an eating place. The eating-place clause ABC attaches to a Type 47 is simply absent from the Type 48 wording; what that wording adds instead is a flat prohibition on minors. The scrutiny sits elsewhere. Background checks on a Type 48 are the high-scrutiny kind, and where a room programmes entertainment, conditions come attached to that too.

  • Bars and nightclubs
  • Minors prohibited on the premises
  • High-scrutiny background checks
  • Strict entertainment conditions
