Who we license

Liquor licence requirements by business type

Different businesses carry vastly different licensing requirements. We work in the nuances of each.

Restaurants Runs on a Type 41 or a Type 47 — a decision worth making before an offer goes in, not after. Bars & nightclubs Rooms where the drink is the business rather than the accompaniment, which is what puts them on a Type 48 rather than a restaurant licence. Hotels One property, several outlets. Restaurant, room service and portable bars are licensed together rather than one at a time. Liquor stores A Type 21 storefront, where the licence is the business rather than one feature of it. Grocery stores Beer and wine, off-sale, held alongside everything else on the shelves. For a multi-site grocer the same question comes up at every address. Convenience stores One counter, a small footprint, and a licence that stays with the site when the store changes hands. Franchise operators The licence follows the brand model. Service style and site requirements decide it, franchise by franchise. Event venues Hosted events are often served under a special or daily licence rather than a permanent one attached to the building.

The classifications

California liquor licence types explained

Wording below is the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control's own, not a paraphrase.

TYPE 21

Type 21 — Off-Sale General

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for consumption off the premises where sold.

TYPE 47

Type 47 — On-Sale General, Eating Place

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for on-premises consumption; beer and wine for off-premises consumption. Must maintain bona fide eating place status.

TYPE 48

Type 48 — On-Sale General, Public Premises

Authorises the sale of beer, wine and distilled spirits for on-premises consumption; beer and wine for off-premises consumption. Minors prohibited.

Liquor licensing resources

Four things worth understanding before you buy, sell or move a California licence.

01 — Classification

Understanding California liquor licence types

Classification is decided once, at the start, and is expensive to unwind. The wrong one costs delay, the price of the licence you actually needed, and the freedom to trade as planned.

Review classifications

02 — Pricing mechanics

What affects liquor licence pricing

The state caps how many general licences a county may hold, tied to its population rather than to demand. Where operators outgrow that ceiling, value moves — county by county, not statewide.

Market analysis

03 — Route to market

Buying an existing licence vs. applying for a new one

Where a county’s allocation is already taken, the only working route is the resale market — a finite pool released when an owner exits, with more than one buyer waiting.

04 — Site eligibility

Why zoning and CUP approval matter

A licence and a premises are approved separately, and a clean licence does not make a site eligible. Two addresses on the same street can return different answers.

Common questions

California liquor licence FAQs

How do I buy a liquor licence in California?
You typically purchase an existing licence from a current holder, since most types are capped. The sale is negotiated, the licence placed into escrow, and a transfer application submitted to the ABC. Background checks, public notices and local approvals may also apply.
How much does a California liquor licence cost?
It varies widely by type and location — a Type 21 can run from $30,000 to over $500,000 in high-demand areas. Escrow fees, transfer fees, legal assistance and local permits are additional.
Do I need city approval before transferring a licence?
Yes. Most transfers need local city or county approval, frequently including zoning clearance and sometimes a Conditional Use Permit. Authorities weigh location, community impact and proximity to schools or churches.
How long does a transfer take?
Roughly 60 to 120 days. Background checks, application completeness, local government approvals and any filed protests all move the timeline.
